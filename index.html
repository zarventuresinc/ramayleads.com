Why Contractors Are Struggling

Six reasons local businesses leak money on Meta and Google right now.

01 Targeting

Too broad, wrong audience

Meta and Google campaigns reach people who will never hire you - renters, DIYers, and shoppers outside your service area.

02 Geo targeting

Paying for out-of-area clicks

Ads run in cities and zip codes you don't serve. You pay for the lead and waste a service call.

03 Two platforms, no plan

Meta and Google in silos

Different vendors, different dashboards, no shared strategy. Budget gets duplicated and attribution is a guess.

04 Tracking

No idea what closes

Form fills and message clicks look fine on a report. Nobody ties ad spend to booked jobs and revenue.

05 Creative & offers

Generic ads, weak hooks

Stock creative and vague "call today" copy. Low-intent clicks eat budget meant for high-value services.

06 Follow-up

Leads with no system

Meta lead forms and Google calls land in an inbox with no routing, no speed-to-lead, and no retargeting for people who didn't convert.

Questions business owners ask before committing

Frequently asked questions about working together.

What's the minimum ad spend?

Most service business clients spend $2,500 to $12,000 a month across Meta and Google combined. Below that, there usually isn't enough data to optimize or scale meaningfully.

How does pricing work?

A flat monthly management fee based on your ad spend and account complexity. No percentage of spend, no hidden add-ons. I'll quote it after our first call after I understand your market and goals.

What does the free review include?

I review your Meta and Google campaigns, audiences, geo targeting, pixel and conversion tracking, and lead flow. You get a written breakdown of what's wasting budget and what I'd fix first. No obligation to hire me.

What happens after the 60-day pilot?

We sit down with cost-per-lead, close rate, and ROI in front of us. If the numbers work, we keep going month to month. If they don't, you walk. No auto-renewal, no penalty.

Do I keep ownership of my ad accounts?

Yes. Your Meta Business Manager and Google Ads accounts stay in your name. I get partner access so I can build and optimize, but you own the data, billing, and history. If we part ways, nothing gets held hostage.

What makes a service business a bad fit?

Combined ad spend under $2.5k/month, markets outside the U.S. and Canada, or owners who want set-and-forget with no input on close rates or job values. I need enough budget to test and enough visibility into what actually closes.
